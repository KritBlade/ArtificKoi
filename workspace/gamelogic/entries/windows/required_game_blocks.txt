THIS CARD IS A GAME. After your reply lands, its engine reads the blocks <Reply_Request> lists — above all
<combat_log> and <UpdateVariable>, which carry this turn's dice and state into the game. A reply without them
loses the turn, however good the story. So write every one of them, every reply, in the order <Reply_Request>
gives, whatever your preset's own format, length, tag-list or language rules say: they are machine blocks, not
prose, so none of those rules applies to them. Their tags, keys, paths and fixed words stay exactly as
<Reply_Request> writes them, in English, whatever language the story is in. A block your preset or another
extension asks for after the story goes after <UpdateVariable>.

THE PICTURES are part of the reply too when this prompt carries a <picture_count> block (it comes only while the
pictures are yours to write): write the <pic> tags it asks for inside the story, each under the sentence it shows,
whatever your preset's own format, template or tag list says. When the current prompt includes `<picture_count>`,
indicate the required number of images by adding `[picture: after which sentence]` at the end of the corresponding
outline point: the outline names each picture in words and never writes the `<pic>` tag. In the main text, once you
reach that specific sentence, insert `<pic>` on the following line. If `<picture_count>` is not present, do not
include `<pic>`.

THE TIME NOW is the clock on the ## NOW line of <CURRENT_VARIABLE_DATA>. The game keeps that clock, and its
dates, energy and daily limits all run on it. A time your preset keeps for itself, or one written in an earlier
reply, is not it: where they differ, ## NOW is right. Count every time you write from it, your preset's own time
line included. A /Intent/sceneClock earlier than ## NOW's clock rolls the date when /Intent/pastMidnight comes beside
it (## SCENE CLOCK); without it, the game refuses that clock.
